ELA Forensics JPEG compression analysis
Error Level Analysis detects regions with inconsistent JPEG compression — a technique used in forensic contexts to identify locally edited or composited areas. Note: images that have been processed in Lightroom and exported as JPEG (as most in this library are) will naturally score higher than unedited camera originals. This is expected — Lightroom applies sharpening, noise reduction, and local adjustments that create compression-level variation across regions. A high score here reflects editing activity, not AI generation or deceptive manipulation. Use it as a relative signal — outliers with an unusually high score compared to the rest of your library are worth a closer look.

Quality Issues 5107 images · local rule engine
30.4% Busy / cluttered foreground 1551 images · travel and landmarks
Edge density in the bottom quarter of the frame is high, suggesting a cluttered or distracting foreground. Move closer, change angle, or use a wider aperture to blur foreground elements.
18.2% Horizon not level 929 images · travel and landmarks
A detected horizontal line (horizon, roofline, waterline) is tilted more than 1.5°. Even small tilts read as unsteady. Use your camera's electronic level, or straighten in post.
13.1% Subject not well isolated from background 669 images · travel and landmarks
The center of the frame is no sharper than the surroundings — the subject blends into the background. Try a wider aperture (lower f-number) for shallower depth of field, or reframe to create tonal contrast between subject and background.
9.6% Subject too centered 492 images · travel and landmarks
The main subject falls near the center of the frame rather than on a rule-of-thirds intersection. Try placing your subject at one of the four power points (roughly 1/3 from any edge) to add visual tension and lead the eye.
6.3% Highlight clipping — overexposed areas 324 images · travel and landmarks
More than 3% of pixels are blown to pure white (brightness > 250). Highlight clipping is irreversible — detail in those areas is lost. Dial in −1 to −2 stops of exposure compensation in bright scenes, or use spot metering on the highlights.
2.3% Shadow clipping — crushed blacks 119 images · people and portraits
More than 5% of pixels are at pure black (brightness < 5), losing shadow detail. This can be intentional for high-contrast moody shots, but if unintended, lift the shadows or use fill flash. Shooting RAW retains more recoverable shadow data.
